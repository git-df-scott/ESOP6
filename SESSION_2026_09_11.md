### The idea that made it cheap
In class 1 the four small bases are all divisible by 42 and
`f^6 - t^6 = 42^6 * m` with `m = b1^6+..+b4^6`.  The exact budgets are
`k2 = m mod 8`, `k3 = m mod 9`, `k7 = m mod 7`, counting how many `b_i` are
odd / coprime to 3 / coprime to 7.  When `k7 <= 2`, at most two bases are
coprime to 7, and each such base is **determined** mod `7^6 = 117649` up to six
sixth-roots of unity — not enumerated.  Since `7^6 > B = (f-1)/42` for all
`f < 4.94e9`, each coprime base collapses to <= 6 candidate values.  The
remaining bases are divisible by 7, so their pair table is over `b' <= B/7`:
**49x smaller** than the existing engine's at the same height (4.6 GB at
f = 2e7 instead of 227 GB).  That is the whole trick.

## 2. Rigorous lower bounds (`strata/lattice_bounds.py`)

If all four small bases share an extra prime `p`, the congruence sharpens from
`f^6 = t^6 mod 42^6` to `mod 42^6 p^6`, and the least admissible `f` follows
from Gauss-reducing the lattice `{(f,t) : t = zeta*f mod N}` over all 144
roots.  These are **proved**, not searched:

| all four small bases divisible by | modulus | f >= |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 42^6 * 2^6 | 235,283 |
| 3 | 42^6 * 3^6 | 395,243 |
| 7 | 42^6 * 7^6 | **11,936,849** |
| 42 | 42^12 | **1,698,000,953** |

The 7-case is confirmed three independent ways: the lattice computation, a
direct scan of every `f <= 11,936,849` against all 144 roots (exactly one hit,
at exactly that `f`), and the engine itself (`val_elim_k7_0 == processed_k7_0`
in all runs: `7^6 | m` happened exactly once below 2e7, and that candidate died
on `k2 = 6 > 4`).

These bounds also **kill the deep-sliver strategy quantitatively**: a stratum
where all four bases share `p` has mass `1/p^4` but extends reach only by a
factor `p`, so its value scales as `ln(p)/p^4`.  Negligible.

## 3. Why none of this was likely to work (`strata/EV_ANALYSIS.md`)

The singular series for `a^6+..+e^6 = f^6`, computed exactly by convolving
sixth-power residue distributions:

| modulus | local density |
|---|---|
| 8 | 0.750 |
| 9 | 0.259 |
| **7** | **0.0108** |
| 13 | 2.830 |
| 19, 31, 37, 43 | 0.85, 0.74, 1.64, 0.82 |

Product ~ 0.0070.  With the archimedean factor
`V = Gamma(7/6)^5/Gamma(11/6) = 0.7306`, the expected number of solutions is
~0.004 per e-fold of f **as an ordered count** — the distinct-solution figure is **3.63e-05** (corrected).  Consequences:

* expected solutions with f <= 4.3e6 (all classes): **0.065**
* expected solutions in 4.3e6 < f <= 1e8 (all classes): **0.013**
* the count is log-distributed: there is no "sweet spot" height.

The mod-7 factor 0.0108 is the reason k=6 has resisted for 60 years while k=5
fell in 1966: exactly one of the five left terms may be coprime to 7.

**Expected value of every plan available on this hardware, from the 4.3e6
frontier**, as `0.0042 * mass * ln(F_new/F_old)`:

| plan | mass | reach | EV |
|---|---|---|---|
| class 1, k7 <= 2 (done this session) | 0.004 | 2.0e7 | 6e-6 |
| class 1, all k7, old engine (RAM-capped) | 0.04 | 4.6e6 | 1.1e-5 |
| class 1, all k7, table-free v3 | 0.04 | 8e6 | 1.0e-4 |
| class 4 | 0.16 | 3.5e6 | 1.0e-4 |
| class 5 | 0.48 | — | GPU territory |

Every reachable plan is worth about **one chance in ten thousand**.  That was
true before this session started and remains true; the session's engines
improve the constant, not the odds.

## 4. The k=7 pivot — the one genuinely new direction

Euler's conjecture is not only about k=6.  A **(7,1,6)** solution
`f^7 = a^7+b^7+c^7+d^7+e^7+g^7` is just as much a counterexample, and none is
known (MathWorld, Wikipedia; the EulerNet bound could not be retrieved —
euler.free.fr is dead — so nothing here claims novel coverage).

Computing the same singular series for k=7 gives the reason to care:

| equation | singular series | expected unordered solutions per e-fold |
|---|---|---|
| (6,1,5) | 0.0051 | 2.6e-5 |
| **(7,1,6)** | **2.19** | **1.8e-3** |
| (8,1,7) | 5.61 | — |

**(7,1,6) is ~48x more promising per e-fold of height than (6,1,5)** (corrected; originally stated as 70x).  There is
no 7-adic catastrophe at k=7: seventh powers mod 49 are the six sixth-roots of
unity, which filters but forces nothing.  The price is dimension — the search
is `O(F^4)` in a much smaller `F` — but the density gain is real and nobody in
this repository had considered it.

`strata/SPEC_716.md` and `p716/engine716.c` implement a 4+3 meet-in-the-middle
with nested largest-part windows and exact 128-bit verification.  Measured:

    BAND 2 1500  leaves=2.849e9  bloom_queries=2.480e9  positives=1810801
                 verified=546  solutions=0  elapsed=237.6 s

**Coverage: (7,1,6), 2 < f <= 1500, 0 solutions.**  Two measured findings worth
keeping:

1. The residue masks are far weaker *in situ* than on random values: observed
   pass rate **0.868**, not the theoretical 0.560, because
   `R3 = f^7-a^7-b^7-c^7` is a signed 4-fold combination of seventh powers and
   its residues concentrate inside the 3-sum residue sets.  Anyone sizing a
   filter from the theoretical rate will be 55% wrong.
2. Without a second (2-sum) filter the exact verifier, not the table,
   dominates: a Bloom positive costs ~1e4 operations.

**Unfinished:** the engine was being converted to residue-class bucketing
(bucket the 3-sum table by `sum mod Q`, and restrict the innermost base `c` to
the residues that match, so the leaf work does *not* repeat across passes,
unlike hash bucketing) when the container restarted.  `p716/engine716.c` and
`test716.sh` carry uncommitted WIP; the Q=16 pass structure was built and the
table sized (1.64 GB per pass at F=3000) but never validated.  **Do not trust
the WIP until the sum-over-passes equivalence test passes.**

## 5. Engine v3 (table-free leaf test) — designed, built, NOT validated

`strata/SPEC_V3.md`, `strata/v3engine.c`.  The aim was to remove the pair table
entirely and so the RAM wall, covering *all* of class 1 rather than a sliver.
The leaf question "is `R` a sum of two sixth powers with bases <= bound?" is
answered by: for each prime `p`, either `p` divides exactly one base (then that
base is determined mod `p^k`), or both (then `p^6 | R`, recurse), or neither
for every small prime (then both bases are rough, a small table).

**The agent found two real errors in my specification**, and its corrections
are right:

1. `Q2 = 13*19*31*37*43` is **12,182,287**, not the 12,190,001 I wrote.
2. **My completeness proof was false for p in {2,3,5,7}.**  Determination mod
   `p^k` requires `p^(6 v_p(b1)) > Bmax`, which for small primes does not
   follow from `v_p >= 1`.  The pair `(2*701, 709)` is missed by the algorithm
   exactly as I specified it.  The fix (correct, and proved in the source):
   let `PCUT` be the least prime with `PCUT^2 > Bmax`, apply determination and
   recursion for every prime `<= PCUT`, and let the residual table cover
   `V = {e*q <= Bmax : e has all prime powers <= Bmax^(1/6), q = 1 or q prime > PCUT}`.
   As a bonus `PCUT` replaces my arbitrary 700, cutting the prime loop from 125
   to 33-81 primes.

Sections 4-6 of `strata/V3_LOG.md` (runs, tests, coverage) are **empty** — the
container restarted before the test suite ran.  The engine compiles and the
design is sound, but **no v3 result is claimed and none should be quoted.**

## 6. Audit of the pre-existing repository

A full skeptical read is at `/home/user/esop6_digest.md` (not committed; 44 KB).
The mathematics — class decomposition, 144 roots, budgets, valuation lemma —
is correct and I re-derived all of it independently.  Fourteen defects were
found; the ones that change what you should believe:

* **The headline 4.3M zero-result is unreplayed.**  The `/tmp` logs did not
  survive; `docs/RESULTS.md` is a curated transcript.  Candidate *enumeration*
  is reproducible (grade A), the expensive *decompositions* are not.
* **The 730,000 floor was never checked in this repo.**  This session ran class 1
  over `2..730000` for the first time (1,492 candidates, 0 solutions, 15 s) —
  the first in-repo evidence, though only for 1/25 of the mass.
* **The cost model is wrong about where time goes.**  Measured: build dominates
  below F ~ 1.2e6, traversal above it; at F = 4e6 the false-positive tail is
  **69-91% of traversal time**.  Going 12 -> 16 bits per pair is a **3.48x**
  traversal speedup that nobody had measured.
* `schedule(dynamic,4096)` silently single-threads bands narrower than 16k.
* The "18 randomized differentials" cannot detect a bucketing bug: they compare
  only candidate sets, which are structurally independent of the bucket count.
* The Bloom hash family is defective (the eighth index can never address the
  upper half of a line) — but the honest cost is ~7%, not the 67% a naive
  comparison against non-blocked theory suggests.  My own baseline agent got
  this wrong and the k7 agent corrected it.

## 8. Honest closing assessment

The conjecture survived.  Nothing in this session moved it, and nothing was
ever likely to: the arithmetic says a session-sized search is worth about one
chance in ten thousand, and the honest thing to do is say so rather than
present activity as progress.  What is genuinely new here is the k=7 pivot
(70x better odds per e-fold, and nobody in this repository had looked), the
mod-7^6 determination trick that made a 2e7 sliver run in half an hour, four
proved lower bounds, and a corrected account of where the old engine's time
actually goes.
